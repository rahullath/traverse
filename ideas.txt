3) opt-in dopamine settings - 

users can enter the general sources of dopamine 
Instead of framing it as good vs bad sources, treat them as regulation tools.

For example the profile could simply ask:

When you feel stuck, what do you usually reach for?

Options might include things like:

scrolling

nicotine

caffeine

gaming

food

exercise

talking to someone

music

pets

No judgment. Just observation.

The app doesn’t say “don’t do this”.

It only uses the info to offer parallel options.

Example interaction:

User opens app at 4:30 PM in shutdown state.

The app might say something like:

People often reach for [scrolling] around this time.

Before that, try one small reset:
• step outside for 3 minutes
• drink water
• stand up and stretch

(perhaps not something as generic and boring as these things, cause any artificial dopamine source is better than "stand up and stretch" or "drink water")

If they still scroll afterward, the app doesn’t care.

The key is inserting a tiny pause before the habitual loop.

Why this works better neurologically

Addictive loops usually follow:

trigger → habit → relief

You’re not trying to remove the habit.

You’re inserting a micro-intervention between trigger and habit.

Even a 30-second delay changes the loop over time.

Another idea that fits your philosophy

Instead of tracking dopamine sources, you could track energy states.

Something like:

Right now you feel:
• foggy
• overwhelmed
• avoiding something
• low energy
• ready to act

Then the app generates the minimum viable action.

This keeps everything stateless and moment-focused.
